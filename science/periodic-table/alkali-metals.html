Soft enough to cut with a butter knife, and light enough to float on water — though it fizzes and gives off gas the moment it touches it. Used in rechargeable batteries.

Why so reactive? Every alkali metal has just one electron in its outer shell, and it's easy to lose. Losing that single electron is all it takes to react — the further down the group you go, the more loosely held that electron is, and the more violent the reaction.
