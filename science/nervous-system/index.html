Inside a neurone

A neurone is a nerve cell. It is a specialised cell with a long, thin shape, so it can carry electrical impulses over long distances. This one is a motor neurone.

  1. 1Cell body. Contains the nucleus and most of the cytoplasm.
  2. 2Nucleus. Controls the cell.
  3. 3Dendrites. Short branches that receive signals from other neurones.
  4. 4Axon. A long fibre that carries the impulse away from the cell body.
  5. 5Fatty sheath. Insulates the axon and makes the impulse travel faster.
  6. 6Nerve endings. Pass the signal on to a muscle or to another neurone.

Crossing the gap

Two neurones do not quite touch. The tiny gap between them is called a synapse.

  1. 1The impulse reaches the end of the first neurone.
  2. 2The synapse is the gap between the two neurones.
  3. 3A chemical is released. It spreads across the gap by diffusion.
  4. 4The chemical starts a new impulse in the next neurone.

Reflex or voluntary?

Reflex action

Fast and automatic
Coordinated by
The spinal cord, or an unconscious part of the brain
Why
It protects the body from harm
Examples
Pulling your hand away from something hot, blinking, the knee jerk

Voluntary action

You decide to do it
Coordinated by
The cerebrum, the conscious part of the brain
Speed
Slower, because you have to think first
Examples
Catching a ball, writing, speaking

The path of a reflex is called a reflex arc: stimulus → receptor → sensory neurone → relay neurone → motor neurone → effector → response.
