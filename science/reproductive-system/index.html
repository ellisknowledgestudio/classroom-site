Reproductive System Explorer

Your reproductive system is the set of organs that can make a baby. Tap any part to see what it does, then follow the journey from an egg cell to a newborn baby. Ages 11-14.

Sex cells

Sex cells are also called gametes. The male gamete is the sperm cell. The female gamete is the egg cell. Each one is a specialised cell, with a shape that suits its job.

  1. 1Head. Contains the nucleus, which carries genetic information from the father. The tip has enzymes that help the sperm get into the egg.
  2. 2Middle piece. Packed with mitochondria. They release the energy the sperm needs to swim.
  3. 3Tail. Whips from side to side to push the sperm along.
  4. 4Nucleus. Carries genetic information from the mother.
  5. 5Cytoplasm. Contains a store of food for the first few cell divisions.
  6. 6Jelly coat. A layer round the egg. It changes after one sperm gets in, so no others can follow.

Not drawn to scale. An egg is about 0.1 mm across, which is just big enough to see. A sperm is about 0.05 mm long, and most of that is tail.

Puberty

Puberty is the time when a child's body changes into an adult's body and the reproductive system starts to work. It is started by hormones. It often begins between the ages of 8 and 14, and everyone develops at their own pace.

The menstrual cycle

From puberty, the lining of the uterus gets ready for a pregnancy about once a month. If no egg is fertilised, the lining is lost and the cycle starts again. A cycle lasts about 28 days, but it varies from person to person.

Day of the cycle. The dark red shape shows how thick the lining of the uterus is.

  1. 1Period. The lining of the uterus breaks down. It leaves through the vagina with a little blood. This is menstruation.
  2. 2The lining builds up. It grows thick and spongy, with lots of blood vessels. An egg gets ready in an ovary.
  3. 3Ovulation. An ovary releases an egg into the oviduct.
  4. 4The lining stays thick. It is ready for an embryo. If no embryo arrives, the lining breaks down and the next cycle begins.

Hormones control the cycle. Oestrogen makes the lining build up, and progesterone keeps it thick. Periods usually start between the ages of 10 and 15. They stop for good at the menopause, at around the age of 50.

A baby in the uterus

From about 8 weeks until birth, a developing baby is called a fetus. Everything it needs comes from its mother through the placenta.

  1. 1Placenta. An organ that grows in the lining of the uterus. Substances pass between the mother's blood and the fetus's blood here.
  2. 2Umbilical cord. Joins the fetus to the placenta. Blood vessels run through it.
  3. 3Amniotic fluid. A bag of watery liquid that cushions the fetus from bumps.
  4. 4Fetus. By the end of pregnancy it usually lies with its head down, ready to be born.
  5. 5Uterus wall. Thick muscle. It contracts during birth.
  6. 6Cervix. A ring of muscle that stays closed until birth.
