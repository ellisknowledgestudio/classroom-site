Skeleton and Muscles Explorer

Your skeleton holds you up, protects your organs and lets you move. Tap any bone to see what it does, then tour the joints and find out how muscles pull on bones. Ages 11-14.

Muscles work in pairs

A muscle is joined to a bone by a tendon. When a muscle contracts, it gets shorter and fatter and pulls on the bone. A muscle can pull, but it cannot push. So another muscle is needed to pull the bone back.

  1. 1Biceps
  2. 2Triceps
  3. 3Tendon
  4. 4Humerus
  5. 5Elbow (a hinge joint)

Two muscles that work against each other are called an antagonistic pair. The force of a muscle is measured in newtons (N).

Inside a joint

A joint is a place where two bones meet. This is a joint that can move, such as the knee, cut open so you can see inside.

  1. 1Bone. Hard and strong. It contains calcium.
  2. 2Bone marrow. Soft tissue inside the bone. It makes blood cells.
  3. 3Cartilage. A smooth layer on the end of each bone. It stops the bones rubbing together and absorbs shocks.
  4. 4Synovial fluid. An oily liquid that lets the joint move easily.
  5. 5Ligament. A strong, slightly stretchy band that holds the two bones together.
