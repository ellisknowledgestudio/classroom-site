Respiratory System Explorer

Your respiratory system gets oxygen into your blood and takes carbon dioxide out. Tap any part to see what it does, then follow a breath of air from your nose down to the tiny air sacs in your lungs. Ages 11-14.

Breathing: moving air in and out

Your lungs have no muscle of their own. The diaphragm and the muscles between your ribs change the size of your chest, and that moves the air.

Air always moves from higher pressure to lower pressure. When your chest gets bigger, the pressure in your lungs falls below the pressure of the air outside, so air is pushed in.

Gas exchange: inside an alveolus

Gases move by diffusion, from where there is more of them to where there is less. Fresh air has more oxygen than the blood arriving at the lungs, so oxygen moves into the blood. That blood has more carbon dioxide than the air, so carbon dioxide moves out.

  1. 1Alveolus (air sac). Filled with fresh air each time you breathe in.
  2. 2A wall one cell thick. Gases only have a very short distance to travel.
  3. 3Red blood cells. Pick up the oxygen and carry it away, so more keeps moving in.
  4. 4Capillary. A tiny blood vessel wrapped closely around the alveolus.
