Senses Explorer

Your sense organs tell your brain what is happening around you. Pick a sense organ and tap any part to see what it does, then follow light, sound, smells, tastes and touch all the way to the brain. Ages 11-14.

Scientists count more than five senses. Balance is detected by the semicircular canals in the ear, and the skin has separate receptors for touch, pain and temperature.

Your pupils change size

The iris is a ring of muscle. It changes the size of the pupil to control how much light reaches the retina. Choose a button and watch.

This is a reflex. It happens automatically, without you thinking about it.

What can you hear?

The pitch of a sound depends on its frequency. Frequency is the number of vibrations each second and it is measured in hertz (Hz). The range of frequencies an animal can hear is called its hearing range. These ranges are approximate.

Elephant
16 Hz to 12,000 Hz
Human
20 Hz to 20,000 Hz
Dog
67 Hz to 45,000 Hz
Cat
45 Hz to 64,000 Hz
Bat
2,000 Hz to 110,000 Hz
Dolphin
75 Hz to 150,000 Hz
10 Hz100 Hz1,000 Hz10,000 Hz200,000 Hz

Humans hear from about 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz. Sound above 20,000 Hz is called ultrasound. Sound below 20 Hz is called infrasound. The top of your hearing range gets lower as you get older.

Listening to sounds above about 85 dB for a long time can damage the hair cells in the cochlea. The damage is permanent. Ear defenders and lower headphone volume protect your hearing.

Taste and smell work together

Your taste buds can detect only five basic tastes. Everything else that you call the flavour of food comes from your sense of smell.

Umami

Example
Savoury foods such as cheese, meat and mushrooms

Try it: hold your nose and eat a piece of apple, then let go. With your nose blocked you taste only sweet and sour. An old idea says that each part of the tongue detects one taste. It is not true.

Which skin is most sensitive?

Touch a friend's skin gently with two blunt points at the same time. If the points are close together, they may feel like one point. The distance at which two points are felt shows how close together the touch receptors are.

Fingertip

Two points felt about 2 to 3 mm apart
Receptors
Very many, packed closely together
So
Very sensitive

Palm of the hand

Two points felt about 10 mm apart
Receptors
Fewer than the fingertip
So
Less sensitive

Back

Two points felt about 40 mm apart
Receptors
Few, spread far apart
So
Least sensitive

The distances are typical values. They are different from person to person.
