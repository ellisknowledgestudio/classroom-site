Excretory System Explorer

Your kidneys clean your blood. They filter out a waste called urea, along with any water and salt you do not need, and turn it into urine. Tap any part to see what it does, then follow the journey from blood to urine. Ages 11-14.

Inside a kidney

A kidney has three main regions. Blood arrives in the renal artery, is cleaned, and leaves in the renal vein. The urine that is made drains away down the ureter.

  1. 1Cortex. The outer layer. This is where the blood is filtered.
  2. 2Medulla. The inner layer. Here water is taken back into the blood.
  3. 3Renal pelvis. A funnel in the middle where the urine collects.
  4. 4Renal artery. Brings blood in to be cleaned.
  5. 5Renal vein. Takes cleaned blood away.
  6. 6Ureter. Carries urine down to the bladder.

Filtering the blood: inside a nephron

Each kidney has about a million tiny filters called nephrons. This is one nephron, drawn much larger than real size.

  1. 1Filter. Blood is squeezed through a knot of tiny capillaries. Water, urea, salts and glucose are pushed out. Blood cells and proteins are too big, so they stay in the blood.
  2. 2Capsule. A cup that catches the filtered liquid.
  3. 3Tubule. All of the glucose, most of the water and some of the salts are taken back into the blood (green arrows).
  4. 4Collecting duct. What is left is urine: water, urea and salts.

Excretion: getting rid of waste

Excretion means getting rid of waste substances made by your body's cells. The kidneys are not the only organs that do it.

You make about 1 to 2 litres of urine a day. If the kidneys stop working, a dialysis machine can filter the blood, or a person can be given a kidney transplant.
