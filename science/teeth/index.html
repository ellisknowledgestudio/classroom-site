Teeth Explorer

Your teeth cut, tear, crush and grind your food. Tap any tooth to see what it does, look inside a tooth, then follow a bite of apple and find out how to stop tooth decay. Ages 7-11.

How a tooth decays

Tiny living things called bacteria live in your mouth. They form a sticky layer on your teeth called plaque. When you eat or drink something sugary, the bacteria turn the sugar into acid. Tap the buttons to see what the acid does.

The decay recipe

Sugar + bacteria → acid
Plaque
A sticky layer of bacteria on your teeth
Acid
Dissolves the enamel
Cavity
A hole in a tooth

Stopping decay

Brush the plaque away
Brushing
Removes plaque before it can do harm
Fluoride
Makes enamel stronger. It is in most toothpastes
Dentist
Finds small holes early and fills them

Two sets of teeth

Milk teeth

20 teeth
Also called
Baby teeth
Arrive
From about 6 months old
Fall out
From about the age of 6
Types
8 incisors, 4 canines, 8 molars

Adult teeth

32 teeth
Also called
Permanent teeth
Arrive
From about the age of 6
Must last
The rest of your life
Types
8 incisors, 4 canines, 8 premolars, 12 molars

The last four molars are the wisdom teeth. Some people never get them, so an adult may have 28 teeth.

Looking after your teeth

Brush

Twice a day, for two minutes
When
Last thing at night and one other time
With
Fluoride toothpaste
Why
It removes plaque

Sugar

Less sugar, less often
Limit
Sweets, biscuits and fizzy drinks
Tip
Keep sugary treats to mealtimes
Why
Every sugary snack starts a new acid attack

Drinks

Water and milk are best
Why
They do not harm your teeth
Take care
Fruit juice and fizzy drinks are sugary and acidic

Dentist

Go for regular check-ups
Why
A dentist can spot a problem while it is small
Tell them
If a tooth hurts or feels sensitive

Animal teeth

You can often tell what an animal eats by looking at its teeth.

Herbivore

Eats plants
Examples
Cow, sheep, rabbit, horse
Teeth
Wide, flat molars for grinding tough plants. Sharp incisors for snipping

Carnivore

Eats other animals
Examples
Cat, lion, wolf
Teeth
Long, pointed canines to grip prey. Sharp back teeth that slice like scissors

Omnivore

Eats plants and animals
Examples
Human, bear, pig
Teeth
A mixture: incisors, canines and molars

Humans are omnivores. That is why you have teeth for cutting, tearing, crushing and grinding.
