What Is Electricity?

Light a bulb, follow electricity from the power station to your plug, sort the appliances and learn how to stay safe. Ages 7-11.

Electricity is energy on the move

Electricity is a kind of energy. It can make things light up, heat up, move and make sounds. Inside a metal wire are tiny particles called electrons. A cell (some people call it a battery) pushes them round, and they carry energy to the bulb.

Electricity only flows when there is a complete loop with no gaps. We call this loop a circuit. A switch makes a gap in the loop, or closes it.

Try it: tap the switch to close the gap. Watch the dots show electricity flowing round the circuit.

Did you know?

  • Lightning is a giant spark of electricity in the sky.
  • Your brain sends tiny electrical messages along your nerves to move your body.
  • Rub a balloon on your jumper and it sticks to the wall. That is static electricity.

Where does electricity come from?

Most of our electricity is made in power stations and wind farms, then sent along cables to our homes. This is called mains electricity.

And batteries?

A cell stores chemical energy and turns it into electricity when it is in a circuit. Cells are small and portable, but they run out. Rechargeable cells, like the ones in phones and tablets, are filled up again from the mains.

Mains or battery?

Does each one plug into a socket (mains) or run on batteries? Choose for each one.

Some things use both! A phone or tablet runs on its battery, but you charge the battery from the mains.

Safe or danger?

Mains electricity is about 230 volts. That is over 150 times more than a 1.5 V cell, and it can kill. Decide whether each one is safe or dangerous.

⚠

Golden rule: only experiment with cells and batteries. Never experiment with plugs, sockets or anything that plugs in.
